Ping Yu

Senior Research Scientist

Meta FAIR

Seattle, WA

I am a Senior Research Scientist at Meta FAIR in Seattle, where I focus on advancing the reasoning and alignment capabilities of large language models (LLMs).

My research interests focus on advancing the reasoning and alignment capabilities of large language models (LLMs). I have explored how to distill System-2 reasoning into LLMs, design alignment strategies such as training models to act as judges and integrating heterogeneous reward signals to improve GRPO, and develop methods for LLM self-improvement through iterative refinement. I am also interested in data-centric approaches, including generating and curating high-quality synthetic data to enhance pre-training and fine-tuning.

My current research focuses on long-horizon credit assignment for LLM-based agents and RL-trained models, particularly designing more informative and structured reward signals for agentic tasks with tool use and multi-step planning.

selected publications

  1. Moving Beyond Binary Verifiable Reward
    Leitian Tao, Ilia Kulikov, Jason Weston, and 1 more author
    2025
    Abs

    Current research on reasoning tasks mainly focuses on verifiable rewards. We studied whether using verifiable answers for GRPO can help with hard-to-verify reasoning tasks, and explored whether including hard-to-verify training data is necessary. We proposed combining reward model signals with rule-based signals for model training.

  2. Distilling System 2 into System 1
    Ping Yu, Jing Xu, Jason Weston, and 1 more author
    2024
    Abs PDF

    Investigate self-supervised methods to ’compile’ (distill) higher quality outputs from System 2 techniques back into LLM generations without intermediate reasoning token sequences, as this reasoning has been distilled into System 1.

  3. CoT-Self-Instruct: Building High-Quality Synthetic Prompts for Reasoning and Non-Reasoning Tasks
    Ping Yu, Jack Lanchantin, Tianlu Wang, and 1 more author
    2025
    Abs PDF

    Proposed CoT-Self-Instruct, a synthetic data generation method that uses Chain-of-Thought reasoning and automatic filtering to create high-quality training data. Achieves state-of-the-art results on verifiable reasoning benchmarks.

  4. RIP: Better Models by Survival of the Fittest Prompts
    Ping Yu, Weizhe Yuan, and others
    In ICML, 2025
    Abs PDF

    Proposed Rejecting Instruction Preferences (RIP), a data filtering method that evaluates training prompts via rejected response quality and reward gap. Achieved +9.4% AlpacaEval2, +8.7% Arena-Hard, +9.9% WildBench gains.

  5. Self-Alignment with Instruction Backtranslation
    Xian Li, Ping Yu, Chunting Zhou, and 1 more author
    In ICLR (Oral), 2024
    Abs PDF

    Developed instruction backtranslation, a scalable method to train instruction-following LLMs by automatically generating and curating instruction-response pairs from web text.

  6. ReStrain: Reinforcement with Self-Restraint Training on Reasoning Tasks
    Zhaoning Yu, Zhaolun Su, Haozhu Wang, and 3 more authors
    2025
    Abs

    Introduced RESTRAIN, a self-penalizing RL framework that transforms unlabeled data into training signals by penalizing overconfident or low-confidence rollouts while preserving promising reasoning chains.

  7. Shepherd: A Critic for Language Model Generation
    Tianlu Wang, Ping Yu, Ellen Tan, and 1 more author
    2023
    Abs PDF

    Introduced Shepherd, a 7B-parameter LLM tuned to critique and refine model outputs using a curated feedback dataset. Achieved 53-87% win rates against competitive alternatives.

  8. J1: Incentivizing Thinking in LLM-as-a-Judge via Reinforcement Learning
    Chenxi Whitehouse, Tianlu Wang, Ping Yu, and 1 more author
    2025
    Abs PDF

    Introduced J1, a reinforcement learning approach for training LLM-as-a-Judge with verifiable rewards that incentivize reasoning and reduce bias. Outperforms all existing 8B/70B models.

  9. Self-Taught Evaluators
    Tianlu Wang, Ilia Kulikov, Olga Golovneva, and 2 more authors
    2024
    Abs PDF

    Proposed Self-Taught Evaluator, a synthetic-data approach for training LLM-as-a-Judge without human labels. Improved LLaMA3-70B-Instruct from 75.4 to 88.3 on RewardBench.
